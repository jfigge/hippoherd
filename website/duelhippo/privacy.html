Privacy

Duel Hippo · last updated 27 November 2026

Duel Hippo has no accounts, no advertising, no analytics and no tracking. It does not sell or share anything about you, and nothing it sends is used to follow you across other apps or websites. What it does keep and send is listed here in full. It is the same in the browser, the desktop app and the iPhone app.

On your device

The game keeps these in its own storage on your device, and nowhere else unless a section below says so:

  • a random player id, made the first time the game runs. It is not tied to your name, your device or any account, and it is never shown to anyone;
  • the three-letter initials and the match name you choose, if you choose them;
  • your settings, best scores, obstacle-course times and the scoreboards of the team games you have played.

Clearing the game's site data (in the browser), uninstalling the app, or deleting its data removes all of it.

The one-player scoreboard

When a one-player game ends and you have entered initials, the game sends your player id, initials, score, game mode and plane type to Duel Hippo's scoreboard server. The server keeps one row per player id: your best score, with its mode, plane and time. Everyone can see the top ten and their own place, showing initials, score, mode and plane only. Player ids are never shown or given out. Until you enter initials, nothing is sent; the game only reads the board.

Team games

A team game is played with people you share a four-letter room code with. The other players in the room see your match name (or your initials), your plane, your score and what you do in the match. Instead of your player id they get a one-way hash of it, which is used to keep scoreboards for the groups you play with on each player's device. Those group scoreboards are never sent to the server.

To play, the players' devices connect directly to each other where they can, which means each device in a room can see the others' IP addresses, as in any peer-to-peer game. To find a direct route, your device asks a public address server (Cloudflare's and Google's STUN servers) for its own address. Where no direct route works, the match server passes the messages along. It forwards them without reading or storing them, and forgets the room when everyone leaves.

Who runs the servers

The scoreboard and match server runs on Cloudflare, which logs requests to its own infrastructure, including IP addresses, as described in Cloudflare's privacy policy. The browser version is served from hippoherd.com by GitHub Pages, which does the same under GitHub's privacy statement. We do not receive those logs or keep any of our own.

The desktop app's updates

The desktop app looks for updates on GitHub Releases only when you turn the automatic check on, or choose Help ▸ Check for Updates… The iPhone app updates through the App Store and makes no such check.

The phone's motion

Repairing a plane by shaking or turning the phone reads its motion sensors during the repair only, and only after you allow it. The readings stay on the phone.

Children

Duel Hippo asks no one for a name, an email address, a birthday or a location. The most it shows other people is three letters on the public scoreboard, and a match name to the friends in a team game. Initials that spell something rude are refused.

Removing your score, and questions

To have your row taken off the public scoreboard, or to ask anything about this policy, write to hippoherd@gmail.com with your initials and score. If this policy changes, the new one appears here with a new date.
